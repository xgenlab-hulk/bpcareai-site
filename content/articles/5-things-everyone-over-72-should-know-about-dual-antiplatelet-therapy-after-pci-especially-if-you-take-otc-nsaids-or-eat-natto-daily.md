# What Everyone Over 72 Needs to Know About Dual Antiplatelet Therapy After PCI — Especially With NSAIDs or Natto in the Mix

If you’re over 72 and have had a coronary stent placed (a procedure called percutaneous coronary intervention, or PCI), chances are you’ve been started on dual antiplatelet therapy over 72 — typically aspirin plus a stronger agent like ticagrelor. This combination is life-saving: it helps prevent clots from forming inside your new stent and reduces your risk of another heart attack. But as we age, our bodies change — how we process medications, how our platelets behave, and even how foods interact with drugs all shift in subtle but meaningful ways.

Many people assume “more medication equals more protection,” or that “natural foods like natto can’t possibly interfere with prescriptions.” Neither is quite right — especially after age 72. In fact, frailty, slower kidney and liver function, thinner blood vessel walls, and even daily habits like taking ibuprofen for arthritis or enjoying fermented soy foods can quietly tip the balance between protection and bleeding risk. Let’s unpack what really matters — gently, clearly, and without alarm.

## Why Dual Antiplatelet Therapy Over 72 Requires Extra Thought

Dual antiplatelet therapy over 72 isn’t just “the same treatment at a different age.” It’s a different *physiological landscape*. After 72, your platelets don’t respond the same way to drugs — some become *hyper-responsive*, others *less predictable*. Ticagrelor, for example, is cleared mostly by the liver, and liver metabolism slows by about 30–40% between ages 65 and 85. That means the drug may stay in your system longer, raising bleeding risk — especially if you’re also taking other meds that affect clotting.

Then there’s the NSAID factor. Over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen or naproxen aren’t harmless bystanders. They block COX-1 enzymes *in addition* to aspirin — which can blunt aspirin’s antiplatelet effect *and* irritate the stomach lining. Studies show that combining NSAIDs with dual antiplatelet therapy over 72 increases major gastrointestinal bleeding risk by up to 3.5-fold compared to DAPT alone.

And yes — natto matters too. This traditional Japanese food is rich in vitamin K2 (menaquinone-7), which supports healthy bone and artery calcification — but it also *counteracts* the anticoagulant effects of warfarin. While vitamin K2 doesn’t directly reverse aspirin or ticagrelor (which work on platelets, not clotting factors), emerging research suggests high dietary K2 may subtly influence platelet activation pathways and endothelial health — especially when combined with aging-related inflammation. One small 2022 pilot study found that seniors eating >50 g of natto daily had modestly higher platelet reactivity scores (measured by VerifyNow) than matched controls — though more data is needed.

Who should pay special attention? Anyone who’s:
- Over 72 *and* has had PCI within the last 12 months  
- Taking low-dose aspirin (81 mg) + ticagrelor (90 mg twice daily)  
- Using NSAIDs more than 2–3 days per week  
- Eating fermented soy foods (natto, certain misos, or aged cheeses) regularly  
- Has a history of anemia, gastric ulcers, or recent falls  

## How to Assess Bleeding Risk — Beyond Just “Taking the Pills”

You wouldn’t drive a car without checking the oil — yet many people take dual antiplatelet therapy over 72 without ever evaluating how well it’s working *for them*, or whether it’s becoming too much.

Standard lab tests like PT/INR won’t tell you much — they measure clotting factors, not platelet function. Instead, specialized tools exist:

- **VerifyNow Aspirin/Ticagrelor Assay**: A rapid, point-of-care test using whole blood. Results come in <10 minutes and report “aspirin resistance” or “high on-treatment platelet reactivity” — both red flags in older adults.  
- **Light Transmission Aggregometry (LTA)**: More precise but requires fresh blood and a specialized lab — best reserved for complex cases or recurrent events.  
- **Clinical bleeding scores**: Tools like the PRECISE-DAPT score (which includes age, hemoglobin, creatinine clearance, and prior bleeding) help estimate 1-year bleeding risk. A score ≥25 signals high risk — and nearly 60% of patients over 72 fall into this category.

Importantly: platelet function testing isn’t routine — but it *should be considered* for frail seniors, especially after a minor bleed (like prolonged nosebleeds or unexplained bruising), before elective surgery, or if you’ve had a stent complication.

Also worth noting: kidney function matters more than many realize. A creatinine clearance under 60 mL/min (common in 1 in 3 adults over 75) doubles bleeding risk on ticagrelor — yet dose adjustments aren’t always made.

## Practical Steps You Can Take — Starting Today

You don’t need to overhaul your life — just add a few thoughtful habits. Here’s what helps most:

✅ **Swap NSAIDs wisely**: For occasional aches, acetaminophen (Tylenol®) is generally safer — but limit to ≤3,000 mg/day, especially if you have liver concerns. If you *must* use an NSAID, take it at least 30–60 minutes *after* your morning aspirin dose — and never on an empty stomach. Better yet: talk to your doctor about alternatives like topical diclofenac or physical therapy.

✅ **Enjoy natto — mindfully**: You don’t need to quit it entirely. Try limiting to 1–2 servings per week (≤30 g each), and avoid eating it within 4 hours of your ticagrelor dose. Pair it with leafy greens *earlier in the day* rather than at the same meal — vitamin K1 (in spinach/kale) doesn’t interfere with DAPT, and helps balance overall K status.

✅ **Protect your gut**: Ask your doctor about adding a proton-pump inhibitor (PPI) like omeprazole — especially if you’re over 72 and on long-term DAPT. PPIs reduce ulcer risk by ~50% in this group.

✅ **Watch for subtle signs**: Not just obvious bleeding — look for fatigue (could signal anemia), black/tarry stools, frequent nosebleeds lasting >10 minutes, or bruises that spread quickly without injury.

✅ **Keep track of your numbers**: Blood pressure, heart rate, and even simple things like how many steps you take daily or how rested you feel can hint at changes in your cardiovascular stability. Tracking your blood pressure trends can help you and your doctor make better decisions. Consider keeping a daily log or using a monitoring tool to stay informed.

🚩 **When to call your doctor right away**:
- Pink or brown urine  
- Vomiting blood or coffee-ground-looking material  
- Sudden dizziness or shortness of breath with exertion  
- A fall followed by headache or confusion (even if mild)  
- Unusual swelling in legs or abdomen  

## You’re Not Alone — And This Doesn’t Mean You’re “Too Fragile”

Starting dual antiplatelet therapy over 72 is a sign of strength — not weakness. It means your heart has endured, adapted, and responded to care. Yes, the balance between preventing clots and avoiding bleeds becomes narrower with age — but that balance *can* be personalized. Many people over 72 safely stay on DAPT for 6–12 months post-PCI, then transition to single antiplatelet therapy (often just low-dose aspirin) — guided by their symptoms, test results, and goals of care.

The goal isn’t perfection — it’s partnership. With your cardiologist, pharmacist, and primary care provider working together, you can fine-tune your plan so it fits *your* body, *your* lifestyle, and *your* priorities. If you're unsure, talking to your doctor is always a good idea.

### FAQ

#### Is dual antiplatelet therapy over 72 safe for frail older adults?

Yes — but safety depends on careful assessment. Frailty increases bleeding risk, so doctors often use tools like the PRECISE-DAPT score and consider shorter DAPT durations (e.g., 3–6 months instead of 12) for very frail patients. The key is individualization — not blanket rules.

#### How long should someone over 72 stay on dual antiplatelet therapy after stent placement?

Most guidelines recommend 6 months for newer-generation drug-eluting stents — and sometimes only 3 months for very high-bleeding-risk patients over 72. Your doctor will weigh stent type, heart disease severity, bleeding history, and kidney function before deciding.

#### Can I take aspirin and ticagrelor long term after age 72?

Long-term dual antiplatelet therapy over 72 is *rarely* recommended beyond 12 months due to rising bleeding risk. After that, most people step down to aspirin alone — unless there’s a strong reason (e.g., recent stent thrombosis or multiple high-risk stents).

#### Does natto interfere with blood thinners like warfarin — and does it affect aspirin or ticagrelor too?

Natto’s vitamin K2 *does* significantly interfere with warfarin — requiring close INR monitoring. For aspirin and ticagrelor, direct interference is unlikely, but high intake may influence platelet behavior in aging bodies. Moderation and timing matter.

#### Are there natural alternatives to NSAIDs for joint pain in seniors on dual antiplatelet therapy?

Yes — options like topical capsaicin, turmeric (with caution — high doses may have mild antiplatelet effects), heat/cold therapy, tai chi, and aquatic exercise show good evidence for pain relief without increasing bleeding risk. Always discuss supplements with your pharmacist first.
